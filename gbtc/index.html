What GBTC Is

  • Product: a trust that holds bitcoin and issues listed shares; it was the primary way US investors got exchange-listed bitcoin exposure for most of the pre-2024 era. Sponsor: Grayscale. Ticker: GBTC (Nasdaq).
  • Fee: 1.50% — the fee row of the Farside table puts GBTC at 1.50%, the highest in the complex, versus 0.25% for IBIT and FBTC.
  • Structure: a closed-end fund — shares trade on the exchange at whatever price the market bids, which can sit at a premium or a discount to the trust's net asset value (NAV).

Why the Fee Is the Highest

GBTC is a 2013-vintage product. The January 2024 spot ETF launch created a dozen competitors charging 0.15–0.25% (IBIT and FBTC at 0.25%, some of the rest at 0.15%) for the same underlying asset. The 1.50% never came down to match, and because the fee is charged on assets under management every year, the cost gap compounds: holding GBTC instead of a 0.25% product costs ~12.5× more per year, on the same bitcoin. For a passive holder with no structural reason to stay, that spread is the whole argument.

Why the Outflows Are Structural (Historically)

  • The premium/discount engine. Historically GBTC traded far from NAV — at times at a substantial premium to it, and at other times at a discount. While it carried a premium, the closed-end structure had no natural exit: you bought, you held. Historically, when the price moved to a discount, the trust's OTC redemption program (available to large holders, with gates that have changed over time) let arbitrageurs sell shares at the discount and redeem underlying bitcoin at NAV — locking outflows in a loop. The mechanism is historical and well documented; the exact gates and volumes vary by era and are not quantified here.
  • Then the substitute arrived. Once spot ETFs launched in January 2024, holders could exit a 1.50% closed-end product into a 0.25% open-end product with the same asset, no discount game required. That is what turned a product-specific quirk into a persistent fund-level drain.
  • The Farside series confirms it. GBTC is the only fund with a negative cumulative since launch: −$28,149M (Farside cumulative row, as reported on the site's daily flows page) — while IBIT is +$64,810M and FBTC +$10,238M. A fund that is net outflow after two years of a bull market is not having a bad week; it is migrating out.
