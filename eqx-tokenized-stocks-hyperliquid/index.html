What is EQX?

EQX is a builder-deployed DEX (a "deployer" in Hyperliquid's HIP-3 permissionless framework) on Hyperliquid. HIP-3 lets third parties launch their own order books and asset listings on the same infrastructure Hyperliquid itself runs; EQX's listing — "EQX Tokenized NVDA", "EQX Tokenized TSLA", "EQX Tokenized META", "EQX Tokenized MU", "EQX Tokenized SPCX" — is confirmed directly in Hyperliquid's spot metadata (deployer label eqx, EVM contracts on the HyperEVM side).

In plain terms:

  • The tokens are tokenized stocks (RWA): on-chain representations of real shares, issuable and redeemable against the underlying.
  • The venue is Hyperliquid's spot stack: order books settle on the Hyperliquid/HyperEVM infrastructure, so you trade with the same execution quality the perp DEX is known for.
  • The wave is bigger than one deployer. The same infrastructure hosts other tokenized-equity issuers — the "Wagyu.xyz" series (TSLA, NVDA, SPACEX) and an "xStock" series (NVDAX, MUX, SPCXX, TSLAX, SPCXD, including "Space Exploration Technologies Corp."). EQX is the newest and currently the most-listed series, which is why its name is carrying the search traffic.

How tokenized stocks work (mechanism)

  1. Issuance. The issuer holds the actual shares in custody (traditionally with a regulated custodian) and mints 1:1 tokens against them.
  2. Trading. Tokens trade on-chain 24/7 — before the market opens, during lunch, on weekends, through holidays. The price tracks the last fair value of the underlying plus demand for 24/7 exposure; expect a spread outside regular NYSE/Nasdaq hours.
  3. Redemption. Holders can (per issuer terms) redeem tokens for the underlying or cash — verify the exact wrap/unwrap flow and fees in the EQX issuer documentation before sizing a position.
  4. Who you're actually buying from. On a spot DEX you trade against the order book, not the issuer. The issuer sets supply; the book sets your execution price.

What this is not: it is not the equity itself, not an ETF share, and not a perp. Dividend and corporate-action treatment (splits, buybacks, ex-dividend dates) depend on the issuer's terms — check them per token.

How to trade EQX tokens

  1. Fund a Hyperliquid-compatible wallet with USDC on the Hyperliquid/HyperEVM stack (CEX withdrawal → HyperEVM, or buy in-app).
  2. Open the spot interface and select the EQX deployer's book (EQNVDA/USDC, EQSPCX/USDC, etc.) — or trade the mirrored pools on HyperEVM DEXes (e.g. Nest) that the aggregators index.
  3. Use limit orders. Books are thin; a market order on a $3–5M market cap will show you the spread.
  4. Mind the hours. Prices can drift from the underlying outside market hours; that drift is both the point (24/7 positioning) and the risk (overnight gap exposure with no close).
  5. Watch for corporate actions on the underlying (Nvidia earnings, Meta reports, Micron guidance) — tokenized holders inherit them per issuer terms.

Risks

  • Thin books. $3–5M market caps mean spreads and slippage; EQTSLA had no prints at all at verification time.
  • Issuer/custody risk. You're trusting the custodian holding the shares and the mint/redeem mechanics. Verify the issuer's backing disclosures.
  • 24/7 drift. Off-hours prices are set by you, not the exchange — both opportunity and gap risk.
  • Corporate actions. Splits/dividends/buybacks are handled per issuer terms; a missed term can mean a bad surprise.
  • Cross-pool divergence. The same token can trade at different prices in different venues (see the EQMETA spread above) — always compare before crossing.
