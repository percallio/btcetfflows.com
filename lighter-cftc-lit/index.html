The CFTC filing — what is being requested

  • DCM (Designated Contract Market): the registration a venue needs to list standardized derivative contracts (perps included) for trade — this is the "exchange" half. CFTC-regulated DCMs already include major futures exchanges; a crypto perps DEX holding one is a first-tier status change.
  • DCO (Derivatives Clearing Organization): the registration for the clearing house that interposes itself between counterparties, manages margin, and carries counterparty-risk obligations — the "clearing" half. Holding both means Lighter would both list and clear its own contracts under CFTC rules.
  • Timing: the plan is to apply in November 2026 (both sources); as of the 11-13 pull this is a stated plan/filing intent, not a completed registration — the CFTC review timeline for a first-time DCM+DCO applicant is not yet published on the exchange (awaiting verification below).

What Lighter is

Lighter is an order-book DEX for perpetual futures built around zero-knowledge proofs — matching happens off-chain (fast, like a CEX order book) while correctness is settled on-chain with zk proofs, and fees/execution are the perps-DEX model (funding rates, leverage, liquidations) rather than spot AMM liquidity. The LIT token is the venue's token (CoinGecko id lighter). Positioning relative to the rest of this site's perps coverage: Hyperliquid's single-stock perps is the spot where tokenized equities trade; Lighter's CFTC play targets the same end-state from the regulated-exchange side.

Why the dual filing matters (and what it does not)

  • U.S.-retail/financial access: a DCM+DCO status is the gate that lets U.S. participants (and U.S. financial counterparties) trade on the venue under the CFTC regime instead of the offshore-entity structure most crypto perps venues run today.
  • Clearing credibility: the DCO half is the rarer prize — clearing carries the counterparty-risk obligation, so a crypto venue clearing its own perps under CFTC rules would be a structural precedent.
  • What it does not do: a filing is a plan. Registration means CFTC review, exam, and conditions; between "applying in November" and "registered" there is a review period of unknown length (no first-time DCM+DCO crypto timeline to calibrate against — pending the filing itself).

What does the CFTC DCM/DCO filing mean for LIT?
