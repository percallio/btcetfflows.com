How We Do It (Method)

Primary sources only. Farside for ETF flows, DefiLlama for TVL, official SEC / investor-relations filings for treasury data. No aggregators of aggregators, no KOL screenshots, no "per reports".

Every page states its source, pull time, and what it does NOT include. If a number is single-source or week-old, it carries a pending verification flag — we say so in the body, not in the fine print.

Numbers are cross-footed against source tables before publishing. Fund-level columns are summed and checked against the market total on Farside; TVL figures are dual-sourced before they go on a page. If the cross-foot doesn't land, the number doesn't ship.

NFA — not financial advice. The data tells you what happened; it does not tell you what to do with any of it.

Why This Site Exists

Most flow data lives on one dashboard (Farside) with no context: the numbers move, but the week's shape — which fund drove it, what it means against the treasury cap countdown, what campaign pays out next — is left to the reader to assemble.

We add the context layer: fund-level breakdowns (IBIT vs the complex), treasury-company cap countdowns (how many days until BitMine's 5% purchase cap binds), campaign calendars (payout dates, yields), and machine-readable endpoints for developers. The dashboard tells you the flow; this site tells you what it is.

How Updates Work

Every hour, the pull script re-fetches the Farside table; if a row changed (a new day posted, a pending column filled in), the affected data pages re-publish with the new pull time in the header line. The hourly re-publish also re-cross-foots the fund columns against the market total, so a source-side correction shows up on this site within the hour.

Treasury pages re-pull price and holdings at the same cadence; filings (10-K, 8-K, IR press) are added as discrete event pages when they land — not folded into a data table until the numbers are checkable.

Nothing publishes on schedule by itself: a page's Last verified date is the date a human-checked pull actually verified the numbers on it, not a timestamp from a cron.

The pull logs keep the raw Farside table as pulled, so a disputed number can always be re-derived from the source snapshot, not from memory.

FAQ

Is this a BlackRock or Fidelity product? No — independent; we track their products (IBIT, FBTC) alongside the rest of the complex.

How often do the flows update? Hourly — the data page re-pulls Farside every hour and re-publishes when the table changes.

Do you publish AUM or NAV? Only where a primary source (filing or fund fact sheet) states it; cumulative flows are always published, AUM is flagged when pulled.

Can I use the JSON in my project? Yes — /data/etf-flows.json is keyless at 100 requests/day; higher volume is the paid API at /subscribe/.

Why "pending verification" flags? Single-source or week-old numbers get flagged in the body so you can tell verified data from data we're still re-pulling — the flag comes off when the cross-foot lands.

Who runs this? An independent desk (byline: Crypto Desk), self-hosted, self-run; the contact paths are the footer line and the Buttondown note field.
