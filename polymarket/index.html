What Polymarket is, structurally

  • Binary outcome markets: each market resolves Yes/No; shares pay $1 if you're right, $0 if you're not; a share at 46¢ is the market's 46% estimate.
  • Chains: Polygon for on-chain settlement plus off-chain matching ("Off Chain" in DefiLlama's taxonomy) — the matching is centralized and fast, settlement is on-chain.
  • Products: Polymarket International, Polymarket US and Polymarket Combos are tracked as separate fee lines — the US product has its own fee schedule and onboarding.

The fee formula (per DefiLlama's protocol notes) matters more than the total:

  • US taker fee: Θ × C × p × (1 − p) — C = number of contracts, p = trade price, with Θ = 0.0695 (effective from 2026-09-16 11:59 PM ET; was 0.06 before that, 0.05 from 2026-04-04).
  • International: users pay fees when they trade binary options.
  • Shape of the curve: p × (1 − p) peaks at p = 0.5 — fees are highest on 50/50 coins and near zero on near-certain outcomes. A "sure thing" at 99% costs almost nothing to take; a contested market at 50% costs the most. If you're scaling into 50/50 markets, price that in.

How to use Polymarket

  1. Pick your product: International site (wallet/USDC on Polygon) or the US product (its own onboarding and fee schedule).
  2. Fund: USDC is the settlement asset on the international side; the US product has its own deposit rails.
  3. Read the market before the price: every market page states its resolution source and conditions. The odds are only as good as the resolution criteria — two readers can legitimately price the same market differently on interpretation.
  4. Trade shares, not "bets": you can enter and exit at any time; your P&L is the price change. Limit orders get you the best fill; market orders pay the spread on thin books.
  5. Manage resolution risk: some markets resolve on specific data sources or time stamps. Check the market's resolution details page before sizing up.

How to withdraw (the part people search for)

  1. International: redeem winning shares (markets settle to USDC on Polygon), then withdraw USDC from your wallet to a CEX (Binance/OKX/Bybit) and off-ramp, or hold on-chain. Polygon gas is small; check your wallet's network settings so you don't withdraw on the wrong chain.
  2. US product: withdrawals follow the US product's rails (bank-linked for most US users) — the flow is different from the international wallet model, so follow the in-app instructions rather than any tutorial that assumes Polygon.
  3. Timing: withdrawals are not instant by nature of the venue — redeem, then transfer, then off-ramp. Budget a step for each.

Risks

  • Resolution disputes. The market's "probability" can be right and your wallet still lose, if the resolution source interprets an edge case against you.
  • Fee shape. The US taker curve penalizes 50/50 markets most — high-conviction trades near certainty are cheap, coin-flips are taxed hardest.
  • Liquidity on niche markets. Top markets (Fed, elections) are deep; everything else can gap. The 24h volume column above is your reality check.
  • It's a market, not an oracle. Odds reflect what traders are willing to pay, including positioning and flow — treat them as a probability estimate with spread, not ground truth.
