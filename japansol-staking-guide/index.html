Route A — Stake SOL for japanSOL (Sanctum)

  1. Fund your wallet with SOL (the amount you want to stake, plus a little extra for fees).
  2. Open Sanctum (app.sanctum.org) and connect your wallet.
  3. Find the japanSOL pool. Sanctum is Solana's LST aggregation layer — the japanSOL token is one of its pool tokens [DefiLlama: "built on Sanctum"]. Select the SOL → japanSOL direction for the pool.
  4. Approve the swap/stake transaction and confirm. You will receive japanSOL — the staking receipt that stays liquid and accrues the pool's economics while the underlying SOL is staked [japansol.com: "Stake SOL, receive japanSOL, and stay liquid while it earns"].
  5. Verify in your wallet: the new token should show the JApan7h6… address.

UI note: the official site points to "Stake on Sanctum" but does not document the click path step-by-step; the flow above follows Sanctum's standard pool-swap mechanics. pending verification: exact UI labels may differ — what matters is (pool = japanSOL, direction = SOL in / japanSOL out, contract = JApan7h6…).

Route B — Buy japanSOL on Jupiter (Market Route)

  1. Open Jupiter (jup.ag), connect your wallet.
  2. Set SOL → japanSOL. japanSOL has been priceable and routable on Jupiter since 2026-08-03 [Jupiter price API].
  3. Check the quote price against the SOL peg. At the last pull the token quoted ~$84.50 against SOL $83.46 (≈1.012×) — a ~1.2% premium to the underlying [Jupiter + CoinGecko, 11-12]. Buying above 1.0× means you are paying the market's current premium to stake; a wider premium = pricier entry than the stake route.
  4. Swap. The token should arrive with the JApan7h6… address.

Which route? If you are holding long-term and want the cleanest entry, Route A (stake directly — no premium). If you need size fast or the pool is slow, Route B — but read the premium first.

Exit Paths

  • Liquidate: swap japanSOL → SOL on Jupiter (or the reverse direction on Sanctum). You exit at the market peg — the same premium/discount number from Route B step 3 now works in reverse.
  • Unstake via Sanctum: return to the pool and reverse the swap; availability and timing follow Sanctum's pool mechanics.
  • Planned, not live: "japanSOL on Jupiter Multiply" (auto-borrow against your japanSOL collateral and reinvest) is listed as coming soon on the official site, with its own warning that leverage can amplify losses and market moves may lead to liquidation [japansol.com]. Do not look for Multiply buttons before the site says they are live.

Common Mistakes

  1. Buying the copycat. The pump.fun "JAPANSOL" ($2.3K cap, 1 sell / 0 buys in its last 24h) is a different contract on a different origin [DexScreener]. Symptom: your token address is not JApan7h6….
  2. Paying premium without noticing. The LST trades around 1.01× SOL; on thin hours the quote can drift further. A market buy is not free staking — the spread is your cost.
  3. Thinking japanSOL is a chain or a new app. It is a token on Solana inside the Sanctum layer [DefiLlama]. No new chain, no new wallet network, no bridge.
  4. Quoting an APY you never verified. The official site has an "Est. APY" field that is JS-rendered and did not resolve in our 11-12 pull — pending verification. If you saw a specific APY number in a screenshot, it is unverified until the site's live metric or the pool's on-chain data confirms it.
  5. Trusting the issuer list from memory. The site says two organizations (Allied Architects, DeFi Development Corp.); DefiLlama's listing names three (adding Dawn Labs) — pending verification. For a staking decision the operator question is real; check both sources at decision time.
  6. Holding for the Multiply feature that isn't live yet. It is "coming soon" [japansol.com] — plan your exit without it.

Troubleshooting

  • Swap failed / slippage error on Jupiter: the pool is small relative to the majors; lower size or slippage tolerance, or use Route A.
  • Token not showing in wallet: import the token manually with the contract JApan7h6JoKBXwSGzaNmTanfY3sBWDiirWp2gqZkMGtH.
  • Stuck transaction: check the fee — keep a SOL balance above the stake amount for network fees.
  • Peg far from 1.0 (e.g., >1.05 or <0.95): stop, read the TVL & price data page for the current curve before acting — a hard peg break is pool-stress information, not a discount.
